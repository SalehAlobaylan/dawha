# Bringing Arabic maps into Dawha

Design and local database audit, 22 November 2026. The first deliverable should let a researcher search مطير, see how several sources wrote it, and open the exact evidence for each result. The historical atlas can use that evidence at three levels: a label on a sheet, a reviewed association with a place, and a reviewed historical area. Each level answers a different question.

## What the first six names reveal

Al-Qahtani's 2010 study contains an Arabic map for the study period 1871–1913. Figure 6 has six tribal names and gives no individual tribal boundaries. I compared the six printed forms with base captions for the currently registered Admiralty checksum and with public names in the local development database. The comparison used literal spellings. A missing result measures catalog coverage only. Dickson's 1929 sketch and his 1935 sketch are distinct archival items, each also distinct from Dawha's existing Dickson overview. The 1929 archive record identifies its red line as the old Kuwait boundary. ## The data to keep

Use one stable ID for each occurrence of a name, symbol, or statement in a particular source. Keep that ID when a moderator corrects a spelling or moves a crop on the same image. One tribe may have many occurrences. One occurrence may have competing identifications. An unresolved occurrence must remain citable.

| Record | Required information and behavior | Fit with the current repository |
|---|---|---|
| Bibliographic source | Identify the work and edition, creator or recorder, publisher or archive, call number or permalink, and publication date. Track the original behind a translation, reprint or repost when known. | Use existing `sources` and reviewed `source_dependencies`. A link-only source can exist without local media. |
| Sheet, figure or segment | Give each map or segment a stable ID under its source. Record physical PDF page and printed page separately, figure/folio or video time, and revision. For a local image keep its file ID and digest, dimensions, orientation, and page-to-crop transform. For an external resource keep the original item URL or IIIF canvas and the citation locator. | Add an item beneath `sources`. Bind renderable items to the exact `source_files` row. One book may contain several plates, insets or unrelated diagrams. Preserve `source_page_evidence` for OCR runs. |
| Access and reuse | Record holder, rights statement URL/text, credit, verification date, and permitted actions for this copy. Public metadata, external viewing, serving pixels, and publishing an adaptation are separate actions. Unknown reuse starts with a citation link. Source visibility still governs the audience. | The plate register exposes a license string and checks visibility. The tile, crop, thumbnail, search, dossier and embed paths need the same item-level policy before accepting restricted material. |
| Occurrence | Save the source spelling verbatim, script and language if known, reviewed Arabic display form, and how it was obtained (original Arabic, transcription, transliteration, translation or spoken mention). Store alternatives and who proposed/accepted them. Record a symbol's legend key when interpretation depends on one. | Keep `plate_captions` and their revisions for map labels. Extend their linkage to the item, source locator and reading evidence. Use passages/statements for prose or transcribed speech. Do not require Arabic to be known at intake. |
| Identification and claim | Store proposed target type and ID, alternatives, review status, reviewer and supporting locator. A label can identify a tribe, subgroup, locality, or feature. A substantive claim needs its own subject, relation, period and evidence. | `plate_captions.tribe_id` handles confirmed public tribe links. Existing `source_statements`, `claims` and `claim_evidence` can carry a passage-backed interpretation. Add a typed occurrence link for other targets or unresolved candidates. Current `branches` requires a family ID, so keep an unmatched historical subdivision as a sourced reading until there is a suitable target. |
| Historical time and location | Store the period represented and its precision, the source's wording/calendar, and any explicit year bounds for filtering. Distinguish sheet-wide coverage from a date specific to an occurrence. Keep publication, source observation and digital acquisition as separate fields. A position may be a source pixel, reviewed named place, or projected coordinate with measured uncertainty. | Reuse the existing geographic date windows after review. The register currently returns acquisition metadata as `plateDate`. A missing depicted date stays unknown and appears only in a dated search when the researcher includes undated material. |

For example, the al-Ahsa record would identify figure 6 on printed and PDF page 108, carry 2010 as publication year, 1871–1913 as the study's coverage, and retain **مطير** as the Arabic printed form. The first assertion is “figure 6 labels مطير within its depiction of al-Ahsa.” A reviewer can cite the figure for that assertion without assigning a coordinate, an individual boundary, or continuous occupation throughout 1871–1913. Capture the rectangle only in an identified image coordinate frame. For a link-only item the page-and-figure citation is sufficient.

## Matching names across scripts

1. **Preserve and index.** Preserve the exact printed form and verbatim citations. Derive a versioned Arabic search key using Dawha's existing normalizer, including diacritic, tatweel, alif and final-letter folding. Use separate Latin keys for case/punctuation and reviewed transliteration variants. Keep a shared Arabic/Latin fixture set for the Go and TypeScript matchers, which currently differ on punctuation and digits.
2. **Generate candidates.** Rank exact attested forms and reviewed aliases before fuzzy candidates. Use neighboring place names, era, legend, script and feature type to rank possibilities. Show the reviewer which evidence influenced the suggestion. Treat بنو / بني and آل / أهل as possible contextual variants. Preserve them in the source spelling. The same search key can belong to multiple entities.
3. **Review the identity separately from the reading.** A legible name may still have an uncertain referent. An Arabic caption can be published with a reviewed spelling and no dossier link. Keep disputed matches and alternative readings with their source. An accepted occurrence can add an attested spelling to an entity's search results. Add a general dictionary alias only after checking its scope.

**BARRIYAH is a regression case.** The Admiralty ink reads BARRIYAH. Dawha displays برية. Hamza's 1933 book prints بريه under مطير, and the next printed page lists subdivisions. The existing Arabic key folds the two Arabic spellings together. Searching them should return both locators, preserve the distinct spellings, and identify Hamza as the source of that classification. بريدة should remain a separate city result. Hamza supplies evidence about the name and affiliation. His printed passage supplies no point for the Admiralty label. ## Spatial comparison, disagreement and publication

- **Read before projecting.** A page, image, IIIF canvas or video time can support a reading immediately. Offer pixel-based source comparison by occurrence as well as the existing geographic-point comparison. Only offer a shared map cursor where both items have validated transforms. Store frame dimensions and rotation for each figure or inset. IIIF canvases and Web Annotation selectors provide established representations for external image regions and timed segments. [IIIF Presentation](https://iiif.io/api/presentation/3.0/) · [Web Annotation selectors](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/#fragment-selector)
- **Measure geographic registration per sheet.** Use printed graticule intersections or well-identified stable landmarks spread across the frame. Withhold geographically distributed controls from fitting. Record model, fitting and held-out points, residuals in kilometres, maximum error and coverage. Compare errors to the intended display scale and distance between features a reader must distinguish. Where coverage or accuracy is inadequate, retain a source-only view. A label's pixel anchor locates the ink. Its distance from the tribe's actual area is a separate uncertainty.
- **Compare the same assertion.** Group by reviewed subject, relation, geographic scope, compatible time and, where relevant, season. Spelling disagreement, alternative identities, different dates, overlapping grazing areas and incompatible boundary claims need different review outcomes. Two reviewers on one sheet establish agreement on a reading. Two independent historical witnesses require a separate source-dependency assessment. Show “2 depictions, independence unknown” when appropriate. Absence from a map is an omission unless the source explicitly claims exhaustive coverage.
- **Publish deliberately.** An approved label enters source search. An identified occurrence can appear on a dossier with the source author's wording. A supported, dated relation to a resolved place may become a `geographic_associations` row with an explicit status and claim evidence. Publish a region only when a source actually delineates it and the existing two-moderator boundary review passes. Carry its validity dates to `historical_regions` and keep the locator retrievable. Research answers should cite the exact figure or passage for the predicate they assert.
